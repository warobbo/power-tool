    How to use

    1. Pick a typical van run, or keep Inverter DC feed if you have already sized an inverter.
    2. Set 12 V or 24 V, then type current in amps or watts. You can change the inverter starting figure. A 12 V inverter needs fat cable; 24 V is about half the amps.
    3. Type the one-way length in metres. The calculator doubles it for the return cable.
    4. Keep 3% drop for important kit, or 10% for everyday kit. The big figure is mm² — how UK copper cable is sold. Inverter feeds use thick flexible copper, not thin chassis cable.

    FAQ

    What is voltage drop?

    Voltage drop is the volts you lose along the cable. A long, thin cable wastes power as heat and the fridge or inverter can sag. This page aims for a small drop — 3% for important kit, 10% for everyday kit — then picks the next copper size you can buy.

    Why is the length doubled?

    You type the one-way length — battery to the kit. The return cable is just as long, so the calculator uses twice that for voltage drop. A 2 m run is 4 m of cable in the maths.

    Does the fuse protect the gadget or the cable?

    The fuse protects the cable. If something shorts, the fuse should blow before the cable overheats. Size the fuse a bit above the current you will draw, and not above what that cable can take. The gadget may have its own fuse as well.

    12V or 24V — does it change the cable size?

    Yes. The same watts is about half the amps at 24 V, so you can often use thinner cable. Match the voltage to the leisure system you actually have. Do not mix 12 V and 24 V figures.

    Why does an inverter need such thick cable?

    A 12 V inverter run can draw hundreds of amps from the leisure battery. That current needs fat, short, flexible copper battery or welding-style cable — not thin chassis wire — and a large fuse near the battery. 24 V roughly halves the amps, so the cable can often be thinner. This page is a planning size, not a wiring diagram.

    What sort of cable is this for?

    Copper is assumed. Fridge, pump and solar runs can use ordinary leisure cable. An inverter battery feed assumes thick flexible copper battery or welding-style cable, not thin chassis or thinwall cable. Those thin cables cannot carry inverter amps.

    What are ANL, MEGA and MIDI fuses?

    They are larger DC fuses used on fat cables, often next to the battery on an inverter feed. A blade fuse is for smaller runs such as a fridge or pump. The amp figure matters more than the name. This page does not pick a fuse brand.

    Why not pick a thinner cable to save money?

    A thin cable runs hot and wastes power. Lights can dim, a fridge can cut out, and in a bad case the cable can melt. Round up to the next size you can buy. Cable is cheaper than a fire or a ruined weekend.

    Should I type amps or watts?

    Either. Watts divided by volts gives amps. For solar, the honest figure is often the current on the panel or controller label, not watts divided by 12 V. You can type that current instead.

    Can I use the Inverter watts as a starting point?

    Yes. The Inverter DC feed preset uses inverter AC watts divided by battery volts — the usual cable-chart method. It does not also add inverter waste on top. You can always type a different figure.

    Is this safe to use as an installation guide?

    No. This is a planning estimate only. It is not an electrical design, installation certificate, or a claim of ISO or ABYC compliance. Cable type, temperature, how the cable is run, and the fuse holder all matter. Use a qualified installer for safety-critical work.
