    How to use

    1. Daily energy is taken from Daily Power if you have used it. You can type your own watt-hours instead.
    2. Choose how many days you want to last without charging. Two is a common start.
    3. Pick LiFePO4 or AGM / lead-acid. That sets how much of the rated battery you can safely use.
    4. Sizes update as you type. Refresh the page: your numbers stay on this device.

    FAQ

    What does “days without charging” mean?
    It is how long you want the batteries to last if the solar is poor or you are not on hook-up. Two days covers a dull weekend for many people. Add more if you camp for longer without charging.
    Why is a LiFePO4 bank smaller than AGM?
    You can use more of a lithium battery’s rated size. This calculator plans LiFePO4 at 80% usable and AGM / lead-acid at 50%, so the AGM bank needs to be larger for the same daily use.
    12V or 24V — which Ah number do I use?
    Use the Ah figure that matches your leisure-battery voltage. The same watt-hour bank is half the Ah on 24 V (Wh ÷ 24) compared with 12 V (Wh ÷ 12). Do not mix the two.
    Should I buy the exact Ah figure?
    Treat it as a planning size, then round up to a battery you can actually buy. Weather, fridge run-time, cable losses and battery age all change the real figure. Leave a margin and get a qualified installer for safety-critical work.
    Can I change the daily watt-hours here?
    Yes. Use “Enter a different figure” if you do not want the Daily Power total. Your Daily Power list stays as you left it. Switch back any time to use that saved total again.
